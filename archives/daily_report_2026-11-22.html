AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control
👥 Yiyang Du, Yuxuan Kuang, Qitao Zhao
Frontier multimodal language agents such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated strong capabilities in controlling robots directly via low-level numerical actions without high-level primitives. However, existing benchmarks report only episode-level success rates, which show neither how these agents succeed nor where they fail. In this paper, we present a systematic action-level analysis of frontier agents on direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks. We introduce AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework in which the policy agent states a local intent alongside each action, and a critic evaluates the action's outcome and failure mode against this intent. Our action-level analysis demonstrates the promising capabilities of frontier agents on direct robot control, including closed-loop recovery from local execution failures, reliable semantic grounding across diverse scenes, and strong zero-shot manipulation without task-specific adaptation. It also identifies their current limitations, including task-relevant physical execution bottlenecks such as grasping and alignment, and uneven recovery across failure modes. Finally, we validate our critic on a 400-action human-annotated benchmark and release a dataset of 12,844 annotated actions alongside a fine-tuned open-source critic, Muse-Glimmer-30B, whose failure-mode accuracy rises from 32.58% to 74.24%, enabling low-cost action-level analysis.

AutoMaster: Autonomous Post-Training by Making the Same Model a Better Teacher
👥 Guoxin Chen, Shuang Sun, Fanzhe Meng
Task success alone does not specify which behaviors post-training should reinforce. Even successful teacher trajectories can contain unsupported assumptions, unproductive repetition, or superficial verification. We propose Skill-FT (Skill-Guided Fine-Tuning), which uses natural-language skills to suppress undesirable habits and elicit underrepresented effective behaviors during teacher rollouts, then fine-tunes a target model on the resulting trajectories. We introduce AutoMaster, an autonomous post-training system in which an LLM agent develops these interventions through trajectory analysis, skill design, data selection, and feedback from rollouts and target-model evaluation. Across Terminal, Search, and software engineering tasks with 4B and 9B target models, AutoMaster improves mean performance over Vanilla SFT on successful unguided teacher trajectories by 1.42-8.99 percentage points, with the teacher, task pool, training set size, and SFT recipe held fixed. Together with the ablations and trajectory analyses, these results suggest that behavioral shaping can make a fixed model a more effective teacher. We further find that retaining skill instructions during SFT yields higher mean performance than removing them across all six settings, despite their absence at evaluation and identical supervised outputs. These findings suggest that preserving skill context as part of a training demonstration can support better behavioral internalization.

Multimodal Error Detection in Chest X-ray Reports: A Large Language Model Reading Image Evidence
👥 Yanda Cheng, Bo Wen, Yuhao Lu
Rad-Linter checks chest X-ray reports against image-derived evidence to flag potential errors for review. A pretrained image classifier supplies scores for nine pathologies, and a 1.5B-parameter language model, fine-tuned with LoRA on 1,816 examples, checks their consistency with the report. We compare it with a keyword-and-negation rule baseline given the same reports and scores. Evaluation uses controlled report-score pairings from OpenI, with consistency labels derived from automatic report labels and score thresholds. Each counterfactual quad crosses two real reports with two real score vectors from other patients, creating two consistent and two inconsistent pairs. Every report and score vector occurs in both conditions, so either input alone permits only 50% accuracy. On 131 quads, Rad-Linter achieves 98.5% pair accuracy versus 80.5% for the rule baseline, and classifies all four pairs correctly in 94.4% of quads versus 56.5%. The checkers perform similarly on omission items, including unmentioned and negated findings; larger differences occur on the remaining item types, which require interpreting how findings are described. The same language model without fine-tuning remains near 50% accuracy. On a second set of 181 quads that also includes reports on which the automatic labelers disagree, pair accuracy is 93.4% versus 75.3%. These controlled results show that, given the same image-derived scores, a trained language model detects report-score inconsistencies more accurately than the tested rule baseline, with the advantage concentrated in interpreting how findings are worded.

Democratizing Cross-Project Knowledge for Transforming Water Governance and Climate Resilience: an Agentic AI Ecosystem
👥 Oriol Alàs, Ian Palacín, Iván Cester
EU-funded projects produce extensive evidence in the form of deliverables, reports, and case studies. However, this knowledge is typically scattered across projects and document structures, making it difficult to locate relevant, contextual information when making decisions. This paper introduces an Agentic AI Ecosystem that consolidates heterogeneous project documentation into a domain-aware knowledge base and provides adaptive access through an agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline. Documents from three EU-funded projects (IMPETUS, NATALIE, and InnWater) are reconstructed into hierarchical section trees and indexed with structured metadata. Retrieval is supported by two domain-specific embedding models fine-tuned on domain data (water and climate). On top of this knowledge base, a ReAct-style controller iteratively orchestrates retrieval actions by allocating retrieval budget across domain collections and accumulating evidence until a stopping criterion is met. In a retrieval benchmark over the corpus, the proposed approach improves Mean Reciprocal Rank by 26.7% (relative) compared to a single-domain embedding baseline, indicating more consistent retrieval of relevant evidence to support cross-project knowledge synthesis and climate-resilient decision-making.

HiSA: Sparsifying the Hidden Dimension of RoPE Attention in LLMs
👥 Ambroise Borbely, Mathieu Salzmann, Olivier Verscheure
Attention in large language models (LLMs) scores every query against every key with a full dot product. We investigate whether this dot product must be computed completely for attention to be effective. We propose Hidden Sparse Attention (HiSA), which keeps the query and key projections intact but only computes a partial dot product, following a static, parameter-free rule set by the token positions: attention becomes sparse in the hidden (feature) dimension, an axis much less explored than token sparsity, rather than over tokens as in almost all sparse attention methods. Based on the structure of rotary position embeddings (RoPE), we decompose keys and queries into parts which can be omitted when computing attention. By training LLMs from scratch with HiSA, we find that this partial dot product does not hurt but improves performance compared with the complete product while computing only about a third of the query-key products. During inference HiSA improves decoding speed and shrinks memory footprint. We also extend HiSA to FlashAttention-3 kernels for training and inference. Finally, we motivate our approach by showing that dense attention learns to suppress the attention score parts that HiSA drops. Code is available at https://github.com/ambroise-b/hisa.

One Framework to Diagnose Them All: A Stratified Benchmark for Bengali LLM Hallucination
👥 Asif Shahriyar Sushmit, Shefayat E Shams Adib, Abdullah Al Jubaer Gem
Hallucination research in low-resource languages fragments easily: each newly named failure mode prompts its own dataset, spending scarce annotators on incomparable resources. We argue for consolidation and test it at community scale. Alikbachan is a Bengali hallucination-detection benchmark stratified along three crossed axes: a human-validated cultural-distance band, six task types, and a five-family, 32-mode hallucination taxonomy. It comprises a harvested 2,516-item test set (Phase 1) and a quota-constructed, gold-labeled 5,000-item set (Phase 2). The stratification is diagnostic. Four zero-shot LLM judges vary by over 30 macro-F1 points across strata, and the item-level predictions of 99 Phase-1 and 32 Phase-2 competition systems reproduce the profile: discourse-level omission defeats judges and most teams alike, whereas only a minority over-flag negative controls. The judges' verdict probabilities rank items well (ROC-AUC 0.74-0.95) but are overconfident (ECE 0.04-0.29). An open competition on Alikbachan drew 222 teams and 3,566 submissions, and a single-prompt 32B judge reaches the 57th percentile. Of the 25 teams in both phases, 24 regress on Phase 2 (mean $-17.6$ points) while every zero-shot judge improves, and Phase-1 rank barely predicts Phase-2 rank (Spearman $ρ=0.35$), so a Phase-1 leaderboard alone would have crowned a different winner.

DNA: Unlocking Depth Scaling in CTR Models
👥 Caiyuan Li, Wenjie Wu, Yuheng Huang
Long user behavior sequences are among the strongest signals for click-through rate (CTR) prediction. Cross-attention makes these sequences practical for industrial ranking by keeping compute linear in sequence length. Yet the gains from stacking cross-attention layers taper off: on Taobao search, growing a cross-attention stack from 16 to 28 layers adds only 0.02 percentage points (pt) of AUC. We argue that the key-value side is the bottleneck. Each layer projects the same behavior sequence into keys and values, so adding depth gives the model more reads of a fixed sequence. We propose DNA (Dual-stream Nexus Attention), which lets the sequence itself evolve with depth. At each layer, the request representation (the search query, user features and scene) first reads the sequence. Each behavior token then attends to the updated request representation and uses what it reads to update itself, so the next layer reads a sequence shaped by the current request. The update runs once per request, all candidates share the resulting sequence, and both the reads and the update keep linear complexity in sequence length. At comparable forward FLOPs, DNA improves AUC over cross-attention stacks by 0.08 to 0.26 pt and keeps improving in the depth range where those stacks level off. DNA-4 is deployed in Taobao search. In an online A/B test against the production model, it improves CTR by 0.8% and GMV by 0.5%.

X-split Argus: Learning Reusable History Tokens for Efficient CTR Prediction
👥 Alexander Ploshkin, Nikita Stepanov, Alexey Pismenny
Accurate click-through rate (CTR) prediction depends on user representations that capture both persistent preferences and recent intent, yet processing extensive behavioral histories for every ranking request is costly under production compute and latency constraints. We present X-split ARGUS, a framework for ad ranking whose history compressor and online ranker are trained end to end. The compressor encodes past interactions into a compact set of tokens that are computed offline and reused across candidates, while the online ranker scores ads through cross-attention to these tokens and representations of recent events. Training combines an autoregressive loss for click feedback with distillation of candidate-specific click logits from a larger teacher that scores each candidate using the uncompressed history. The number of compressed history tokens and the length of recent history can be adjusted to fit serving compute and latency budgets. Offline evaluation shows a 0.81% relative reduction in normalized entropy (NE) compared with the production baseline. Deployment in a large-scale real-time bidding system increases online inference cost by 15% relative to the production baseline. An online A/B test against this baseline yields relative gains of 4.65% in a business metric reflecting the monetary value of conversions generated for advertisers and 2.96% in clicks.
