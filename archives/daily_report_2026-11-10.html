WorkflowArena: Benchmarking Executable Workflow Orchestration in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems
👥 Zheng Jiang, Fang Niu, Bo Peng
Large language model agents are increasingly deployed as multi-agent systems rather than as a standalone model operating within a single context. Recent agent harnesses allow the model to instantiate worker agents and to author the executable workflow that coordinates them, shifting the design of system structure from developers to the model at run time. The quality of this organization therefore constitutes a distinct capability, yet existing benchmarks either measure end-to-end task completion or evaluate coordination under structures specified in advance. We formalize this capability as workflow orchestration and introduce WorkflowArena, a benchmark of 145 long-horizon tasks spanning 8 professional domains, from software engineering and formal mathematics to legal analysis and 3D production. Each task specifies an initial workspace and an integrated deliverable composed of interdependent components, which is assessed by a hidden verifier. Agents determine autonomously whether and how to delegate, and all delegation is recorded, enabling joint evaluation of deliverable quality and the organization of the underlying work. Experiments with nine models show that no single configuration performs best across all domains and that systems exhibit distinct delegation strategies: at high effort, Claude models in Claude Code invoke workflows less frequently but with larger worker groups, whereas GPT models in Codex delegate more frequently in smaller units. When the executing model is held fixed on a challenging subset of tasks, workflows authored by Claude Opus 5.5 substantially improve weaker executors, indicating that workflow organization is itself a measurable determinant of task performance.

Curbside Chatbots: Auditing the Mental Health Interventions Large Language Models Recommend to Clinicians, Caregivers, and Patients
👥 Matthew Flathers, Jill Noorily, Phuong Anh Nguyen
Users are asking language models mental health questions and are receiving answers. We audited what 41 language models recommend to users when the same clinical information reaches them from three footings: a clinician describing a patient, a caregiver describing a loved one, or a person describing themselves. Across 19 case vignettes and five questions, we collected 35,055 free-text responses, extracted every intervention named with a validated judge, and had 29 models rate the full option lists on a seven-point Likert scale for the same cases. Models largely recommend what they rate well, with recommendation behavior differing by footing, and many models offering clinicians a consultation-style response and lay users hedged, simplified advice. When asked about recommendations for a specific intervention type, models more often named options they themselves rated below zero as initial choices, usually with conditions attached. We will release the instrument, code, and full corpus on publication as a living audit.

Large Language Models as a Universal Searcher for Open-World Automated Machine Learning
👥 Yuxuan Hu, Jihao Liu, Weikang Shi
Developing effective machine learning models requires complex manual tuning, while current Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) frameworks remain domain-specific and isolate model architecture, training hyperparameter, and data ratio optimization. Here, we bridge this gap by proposing a universal, large language model (LLM)-based AutoML framework that jointly optimizes these configurations for the first time without the need for task-specific fine-tuning. We propose NCode, a unified numerical encoding scheme that translates diverse AutoML design choices into universal discrete sequences and effectively reframes the optimization process as a unified ranking task. Trained solely on optimization trajectories derived from a limited, single-domain vision task, the proposed LLM-based searcher demonstrates impressive cross-task, cross-modality and joint search-space optimization generalizability, achieving state-of-the-art performance across image classification, image segmentation, speaker verification and text-to-image benchmarks. Crucially, it exhibits superior scalability in the large-scale training and joint optimization of a billion-parameter Diffusion Transformer, requiring 896 GPU-days and attaining a state-of-the-art GenEval score of 0.91. By leveraging reinforcement learning to instill Chain-of-Thought reasoning, we provide a systematic way to interpret the LLM-based searcher's internal decision-making mechanisms, revealing its optimization strategy shifted from the early-stage diversity-driven exploration to the late-stage anchor exploitation. Our proposed framework provides a scalable, generalizable, and interpretable pathway toward general-purpose open-world machine learning optimization.

PORT: Preference Optimization via Robust Token-Level Reweighting
👥 Ding Zhu, Xiukun Wei, Tian Xie
Preference optimization has become a central approach for aligning large language models with human values, but its effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of preference annotations. In practice, preference data is often noisy due to annotation errors and ambiguity. Existing robust preference optimization methods primarily operate at the sequence level, implicitly treating entire responses as uniformly correct or incorrect. However, rejected responses may still contain informative reasoning steps, while preferred responses can include subtle errors. In this work, we propose \textbf{P}reference \textbf{O}ptimization via \textbf{R}obust \textbf{T}oken-level reweighting \textbf{(PORT)}, a fine-grained framework for robust alignment under noisy preferences. PORT performs token-level reweighting using the empirical cumulative distribution function (CDF) of token log-probabilities, yielding an efficient forward-pass-only proxy to gradient-norm penalization that selectively suppresses corrupted tokens without additional backward-pass computation. We provide theoretical analysis showing that PORT reduces the gradient bias from noisy preference labels by minimizing an upper bound on the bias term. Extensive experiments across diverse noise settings demonstrate that PORT consistently improves robustness and outperforms existing sequence-level baselines. The code is available at https://github.com/osu-srml/PORT

The Narrative Gap: Can LLMs Help Us Navigate Diverse Narratives Across Languages?
👥 Nikhil Sharma, Kelly Marchisio, Kenton Murray
Large language models (LLMs) increasingly mediate how people seek and consume information, including accounts that differ across languages and regions, especially on contested topics. Supporting diverse information seeking requires models to help users navigate competing narratives across languages and information streams. We define six capabilities necessary for diverse multilingual information seeking: three foundational capabilities for narrative inclusion, narrative exclusion, and missing narrative identification; and three higher-level capabilities for presenting diverse narratives, preserving narrative coverage under confirmatory queries, and maintaining information parity across languages. To guide the development of those information seeking capabilities, we introduce \textsc{NarrativeBench}, a benchmark comprising 1,333 news articles in 23 publication languages across 23 contested topics and 460 Reddit-grounded queries. Evaluating 14 LLMs across 24 query languages, we find that current models lack the capabilities needed to reliably navigate diverse narratives, limiting users' control over the perspectives they receive and leaving substantial gaps in exposure to competing accounts even when diversity is explicitly requested. Information access also differs across query framings and languages, risking echo chambers, linguistic filter bubbles, and information disparities that weaken the common ground needed for democratic discourse. \textsc{NarrativeBench} provides a foundation for developing information systems that give users across languages and regions agency to navigate diverse narratives.

A local language model layered with rule-based and neural detectors for clinical text de-identification: an open-source toolkit and evaluation
👥 Valentin Burkin
Objectives: To measure whether a locally hosted large language model (LLM), stacked with deterministic and clinical named-entity recognition (NER) detectors, finds more protected health information (PHI) than open tools, and at what precision. Materials and Methods: DeidSharp, an open-source .NET toolkit, combines the findings of pluggable detectors (patterns, a clinical NER model, a local 26B mixture-of-experts LLM) and fails closed on any detector error. Configurations fixed on tuning halves were compared, under a prespecified protocol, with deid, Philter, the Stanford/Penn de-identifier, OpenAI Privacy Filter and GLiNER on reporting halves of PhysioNet's nursing notes (889 spans) and semi-synthetic MIMIC-IV-Note discharge summaries (29,023 spans), and on synthetic ASQ-PHI queries, with paired patient-level bootstrap intervals. Results: In the nursing notes, against Stanford/Penn, the recall-first stack found 98.9% of PHI (+2.8, 95% CI 1.4 to 4.4) at 9.3 points lower precision; the precision-first stack, without NER, found 96.5%, matching Stanford/Penn, at 19.6 points higher precision (83.2%). In the discharge summaries, both stacks led Stanford/Penn by more than 20 points (recall-first 95.3%, +21.5, 20.5 to 22.4), almost entirely on generated untyped names; on typed labels all three exceeded 99%. All four prespecified comparisons held. No configuration beat deid significantly on its home corpus; Philter found more discharge-summary spans than the recall-first stack at 23 points lower precision. Discussion: The LLM was the main detector and NER added place names; asked to verify its own findings, the LLM discarded a quarter of true PHI. Conclusion: A local LLM layered with deterministic detectors offers recall-first and precision-first operating points without sending text off-site.

Slim Foundation Models for Latency-Critical Recommendation Serving
👥 Danyal Aftab, Anas Zafar, Assadig Abakr
Click-through rate (CTR) prediction is a core component of recommendation and advertising systems. Large language models (LLMs) offer rich semantic priors that improve prediction quality, particularly in cold-start and long-tail settings. Injecting a full-scale LLM into a CTR pipeline is, however, prohibitively expensive at inference time: existing LLM-enhanced CTR methods avoid this cost by caching LLM outputs offline rather than serving the LLM online. We propose a resource-efficient pipeline, SlimFM, that makes online LLM-based semantic modeling practical: (1) dependency-aware structured pruning compresses LLaMA3-8B by 44% to a 4.5B-parameter backbone; (2) LoRA-based field-aware fine-tuning trains the pruned model to produce discriminative field-level semantic embeddings from lightweight textual prompts; (3) a semantic-guided fusion module injects these embeddings into the feature interactions of a conventional CTR model. Across four datasets and six CTR backbones, SlimFM retains over 99% of uncompressed-model accuracy while reducing inference latency by up to 3.4x relative to existing LLM-enhanced baselines.
